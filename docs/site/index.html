What developers told Stack Overflow in 2023 and 2024

Every year Stack Overflow asks developers around the world about their work and tools. This page tells the story of two of those surveys. A data pipeline cleaned the raw answers and checked them before anything was shown here.

89,184people in the 2023 survey
65,437people in the 2024 survey

Keep this in mind: fewer people answered in 2024, so many counts below are smaller. That is not a sign that developers left. Compare the order of the lists, not the raw size.

Chapter 3

What do developers earn?

This is the part to read carefully. Salaries are grouped by country, years of experience, role, remote work and company size, and a group only appears if at least 5 people are in it. The amounts are in each person's local currency, so only groups from the same country can be compared. The groups with the most data are mostly United States full-stack and back-end developers. In the published data their medians range from 130,000 to 202,500.

Not a salary guide. The survey has no weights and the people who answer are not a random sample of all developers.

Chapter 4

How happy are they at work?

Job satisfaction is scored 0 to 10 and published for 2024 only. For the groups shown, the average sits between 6.74 and 7.46. Germany full-stack developers who do not plan to use AI tools score 6.74, the lowest on the list. United States full-stack developers with a very favorable view of AI tools score 7.46, the highest.

Chapter 5

What about AI?

The 2023 survey did not ask whether AI is a threat or how satisfied people are with their job, so there is nothing to compare for 2023. The old version of this page showed bars of 0% and 100% for 2024. Those were an artifact of how the groups were split, not real opinions, so this story leaves them out until the table is fixed.

How to trust this page

Published snapshot dated 2026-09-18. This page is a plain-language retelling of the same published numbers.

Numbers come from a database release that passed seven data checks and a set of tests before it was published. If a test fails, the last good release stays live. The code, the six failure experiments, and one that got through are in the repository: github.com/swagatskalita092/Stack_Overflow_Pipeline
